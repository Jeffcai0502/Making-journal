## Documentation 
Over the first part of this course, the experiment that influenced me the most was Experiment 3, which focused on real-time data. Working with p5.js and APIs changed how I thought about data visualisation. Before that, I mostly understood data as something that gets organised and displayed, usually through numbers, charts, or fixed graphics. During this experiment, I started to see that data could also be used to create movement, atmosphere, and interaction. Instead of only communicating information, it could shape an experience in real time. Experiment 2 also contributed to this shift, because interactivity made the viewer part of the system rather than someone simply looking at the outcome. Together, these experiments made me more interested in data humanism and in the idea that data can be personal, emotional, and open to interpretation rather than purely objective.

For my project, I want to explore emotional states and private self-expression in everyday digital life. I am interested in the emotions people do not always say out loud, but still reveal indirectly through their behaviour. My proposal imagines a near-future scenario in which digital systems such as apps, interactive platforms, or game environments continuously collect small traces of user behaviour and use them to interpret emotional patterns. In this scenario, the system would not ask someone directly how they feel. Instead, it would respond to the user through atmosphere, pacing, and visual change, creating a more subtle and immersive way of reflecting emotion back to them.

The data for this project would come from a combination of sources. One source would be user-generated interaction data, such as repeated choices, hesitation, input rhythm, movement, or frequency of actions. Another source could be live environmental data such as time of day or weather, which could influence the mood or behaviour of the system. I may also use simulated emotional data during prototyping to test how the system responds before developing a fuller interaction model. Because of this, the project would combine live, behavioural, and partly constructed data. Rather than presenting this data as raw facts, the system would translate it into a visual and sensory experience.

I imagine the final outcome as an interactive screen-based work with a game-like quality. Users would make simple inputs, and the system would react instantly through abstract graphics, shifting colour, particles, sound, or changes in the environment. The purpose is not to create an accurate emotional reading of the user, but to question what happens when a system appears to understand someone through data. I want the project to make viewers feel that they are being interpreted, while also making them aware that this interpretation is partial and constructed. In this way, the work would explore the relationship between behaviour, data, identity, and emotion. I hope the audience comes away with a different view of data: not as neutral information, but as something that actively shapes how we are seen and how we understand ourselves.
