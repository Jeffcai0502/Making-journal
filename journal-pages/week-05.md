## Documentation 

Over the first phase of the course, I found Experiment 3 on live data the most meaningful, especially when working with APIs and p5.js. What interested me was not only the technical side of bringing changing data into a visual system, but also the way live data could shift a work from being a static graphic into something reactive and atmospheric. Experiment 2 also shaped my thinking because interactivity made the viewer part of the visualisation rather than just a spectator. Across these experiments, I kept returning to the idea that data does not have to be presented as neutral numbers or charts. Instead, it can be translated into mood, movement, and experience. This connects to ideas of data humanism from the course, especially the idea that data can be made more personal, emotional, and connected to everyday life.

For my project, I want to explore emotional self-expression in daily digital behaviour, particularly emotions that people do not openly state. I am interested in the gap between what a person feels internally and what a system can infer from their actions. My future scenario is a near-future digital environment in which apps, games, and interactive systems constantly interpret a user’s behaviour and build a picture of their emotional state without directly asking them how they feel. Rather than presenting this as a utopian tool, I want to treat it critically. A system may appear to “understand” someone, but it is really only reading traces, patterns, hesitations, and choices.

The data for this project would combine several sources. The main source would be user-generated behavioural data collected through the interaction itself, such as hesitation time, repeated clicks, movement patterns, choice sequences, typing speed, or pauses. I may also use live contextual data such as time of day or weather through an API to influence the atmosphere of the work. Because this is a proposal and not a finished product yet, I may also use some simulated emotional data during prototyping to test how the system behaves before collecting fuller interaction data. This means the project would combine live, dynamic, and partly constructed data. The data would not be shown directly. Instead, it would be interpreted into changing visual and spatial qualities.

I imagine the final artefact as a screen-based interactive visualisation, possibly with a game-like structure. As the audience interacts with it, the system would gradually shift its visual atmosphere through particles, colour, density, motion, sound, or environmental changes. The viewer would begin to notice that the work is responding to them, but not in a fully transparent way. The provocation of the project is to ask how it feels when a system seems emotionally aware, while also revealing how limited and interpretive that awareness really is. I want the audience to reflect on how behaviour becomes data, how data becomes judgement, and how digital systems increasingly shape ideas of identity and emotion. The intended impact is not to give a final answer, but to create a more critical and embodied understanding of personalised data systems.
